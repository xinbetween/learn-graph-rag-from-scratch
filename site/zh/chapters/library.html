Paper and tool library

Every paper, repository, article and dataset the course draws on, grouped by topic, each with a one-line note on what it contributes and the chapters that discuss it.

How to use this library

The sections follow the course: foundations first, then how graphs are built, how they are searched, how evidence reaches the model, and how systems are evaluated, run and extended. Each entry gives the title (linked to arXiv, GitHub or the official page), the first author, the year, a plain-language note and the chapters where it comes up.

Three ways to read it:

  • Following a chapter. Every chapter's references appear here too. Filter by the chapter's topic, or search for a method name you just met, such as PathRAG or Leiden.
  • Before building. Read the ten papers below, then the open-source tools section. Most practical decisions (which graph shape, which search mode, how to evaluate) trace back to a handful of papers.
  • Tracking the frontier. The 2026 section lists recent preprints. They have not been through the same scrutiny as older work, so read their numbers as claims to test on your own data.

Titles, authors and years for arXiv papers were checked against arXiv. Some papers were renamed between versions; where that happened the current title is shown and the earlier name is noted.

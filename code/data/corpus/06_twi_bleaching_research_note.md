# Research Note: Early Detection of Mass Bleaching at Halden Reef, August 2024

*Tidewater Institute, Reef Ecology Group. Research Note RN-2024-07. Issued 3 September 2024.*

**Principal investigator:** Dr. Ana Ruiz
**Program:** Halden Reef survey, in collaboration with the Sentinel monitoring program

## Summary

In August 2024, autonomous drones operating under the Sentinel program recorded the onset of a mass coral bleaching event at Halden Reef. The first anomalous readings were logged on 11 August 2024, roughly nine days before bleaching became visible to divers during the scheduled summer survey. This note documents the detection, the environmental conditions associated with the 2024 Halden Reef bleaching event, and implications for monitoring.

## Detection

Between 6 and 11 August, water temperature on the reef crest rose 1.8 °C above the long-term August mean and remained elevated. Sentinel drones stationed on the northern and eastern transects then recorded a sharp increase in reflected light from coral colonies, which is an early optical signature of bleaching. Automatic alerts from the Sentinel fleet were reviewed by TWI staff on 12 August, and a rapid-response dive team confirmed widespread paling of branching corals on 20 August.

The detection is, to our knowledge, the first time a bleaching event on Halden Reef has been identified by an autonomous system before human observers. Earlier events, in 2010 and 2017, were only noticed during routine surveys after significant coral mortality had already begun.

## Extent

By the end of August, approximately 38% of surveyed colonies on the reef crest showed moderate to severe bleaching. Deeper sections of Halden Reef were less affected. Acoustic mapping from the Sentinel drones indicates no large-scale structural collapse so far, although fish counts on the most affected transects fell by roughly a fifth.

## Discussion

The bleaching event appears to be driven by a persistent marine heatwave combined with unusually calm, clear conditions that increased light stress. Recovery will depend on temperatures returning to normal before winter. The Reef Ecology Group will continue weekly assessments.

"This event is a loss for Halden Reef, but it is also proof that continuous monitoring works," said Dr. Ana Ruiz. "Nine days of warning is the difference between documenting a disaster and being able to study it as it unfolds."

## Recommendations

1. Maintain the Sentinel fleet on Halden Reef through at least the end of the heatwave season.
2. Extend continuous monitoring to smaller reefs nearer Port Avalon.
3. Share the detection method with regional agencies considering monitoring requirements for coastal reefs.

## Data availability

Temperature, optical and acoustic data associated with the bleaching event are available through the Tidewater Institute reef data portal under dataset HR-2024-08.

# Marlow Dynamics Buys Sonar Maker Deepcast to Lock Down Its Supply Chain

*Maritime Industry Journal, Deals. 17 April 2025.*

Marlow Dynamics, the builder of defense-focused underwater vehicles, has acquired sonar hardware supplier Deepcast in an all-cash deal, the companies announced on Tuesday. Terms were not disclosed, but two people familiar with the transaction put the price at between $180 million and $220 million.

The acquisition gives Marlow Dynamics direct control over one of the few independent producers of compact multibeam sonar. Marlow has bought Deepcast transducers for its own vehicles for several years, and executives said owning the supplier would secure capacity as the company ramps up production for navy contracts in the region.

"Sonar is the heart of every vehicle we build," a Marlow Dynamics spokesperson said in a statement. "Bringing Deepcast in-house means we control our most critical technology end to end, from the ceramic to the software."

## Outside customers

The bigger question for the industry is what happens to Deepcast's other customers. Deepcast has historically sold to anyone, from university labs to survey firms and small drone makers. In a letter to customers seen by Maritime Industry Journal, Deepcast said it would "honour existing purchase orders through the end of the second quarter" but would not renew or sign outside supply contracts after the acquisition closes. Future production will be reserved for Marlow Dynamics programs.

Several customers contacted for this article said they had not been warned. "We found out from the press release," said an engineer at a harbour survey company, who asked not to be named. "There are maybe two other vendors with comparable units, and both have lead times of a year or more."

Analysts said the move fits a broader pattern of vertical integration among defense contractors, which have been snapping up component suppliers to protect themselves from shortages. "For Marlow Dynamics it makes perfect sense," said one analyst. "For the startups that relied on Deepcast, it's a supply shock. Some of them will have to redesign their vehicles, and a few may not survive it."

## Background

Deepcast was founded in 2009 and employs roughly 150 people. Its compact transducers became popular with makers of small autonomous vehicles because they were cheap, low-power and easy to integrate.

Marlow Dynamics, headquartered in a naval shipbuilding district several hundred kilometres up the coast, builds large and medium underwater vehicles for mine countermeasures, seabed security and harbour protection. Revenue grew by more than 30% last year on the back of new defense orders. The company has recently begun marketing a smaller vehicle to civilian and scientific customers, a market where it has not previously been active.

The acquisition is expected to close in May, subject to regulatory approval.

# Deepcast Signs Sonar Supply Deal With Drone Startup

*Subsea Hardware Weekly, Supplier News. 9 August 2022.*

Deepcast, the sonar hardware supplier best known for its compact multibeam transducers, has signed a multi-year agreement to supply sonar modules to Kestrel Labs, a young Port Avalon company developing autonomous underwater drones.

Under the agreement, Deepcast will deliver transducer heads and signal-processing boards that Kestrel Labs will integrate into its survey vehicles. Neither company disclosed the value of the contract, but people familiar with the deal said it covers several hundred units over three years, with an option to extend.

For Deepcast, the deal adds another name to a customer list that already includes university labs, harbour authorities and a handful of commercial survey firms. The company has built its business on selling off-the-shelf sonar to whoever needs it, rather than tying itself to a single vehicle maker. "We are a supplier to the whole industry," a Deepcast sales director told Subsea Hardware Weekly. "Small teams like Kestrel are exactly the customers who can't afford to build their own sonar from scratch."

Kestrel Labs chief technology officer Jonas Vehl said the decision to buy rather than build was a matter of speed. Vehl designed the acoustic array layout for the company's drones, which uses several small transducers spread along the hull, but the company does not have the manufacturing capacity to produce the transducers themselves.

"I know exactly what I want the array to do; I just can't make ceramics and amplifiers in a boat shed," Vehl said. "Deepcast gives us a reliable part so we can spend our engineering time on autonomy and endurance. Maybe one day we build our own, but not this year."

Industry analysts noted that the arrangement carries some risk. Kestrel Labs will source all of its sonar from a single vendor, which is common for small hardware startups but leaves them exposed if the supplier changes its priorities. "Single-source components are fine until they aren't," said one analyst who covers marine robotics. "Supplier consolidation happens in this industry every few years."

For now, the deal appears to suit both sides. Deepcast shipments to Kestrel Labs are scheduled to begin in the fourth quarter of 2022, and the startup says the first drones carrying Deepcast sonar will enter the water early next year.

Kestrel Labs raised a $12 million Series A round earlier this year. Deepcast, which is privately held, employs about 140 people and operates a factory on the southern coast.

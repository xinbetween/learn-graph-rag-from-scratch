# Meet the Team: Priya Nair on Leaving Defense Robotics for Reefs

*Kestrel Labs company blog. Posted 12 September 2023.*

This summer Priya Nair joined Kestrel Labs as lead engineer on Project Sentinel. Priya previously spent seven years at Marlow Dynamics, where she worked on navigation and sensor fusion for underwater vehicles. We sat down with her to talk about the move.

**You spent most of your career at Marlow Dynamics. What kind of work was that?**

Marlow Dynamics builds underwater vehicles mostly for defense customers: mine countermeasures, harbour security, that kind of thing. The engineering is excellent, and I learned a huge amount about keeping a vehicle stable and navigating in murky water. But the vehicles are large, expensive and built for missions I did not always feel connected to.

**Why Kestrel Labs?**

Honestly, it was a Sentinel demo. A friend sent me a video of the drones working over Halden Reef, and I recognised the problem immediately. They were fighting the same drift and navigation errors I had spent years solving, but the goal was to keep a reef alive. I wrote to Jonas Vehl that evening.

**Marlow Dynamics and Kestrel Labs both build underwater drones. Is it strange to switch to a competitor?**

A little. Marlow Dynamics does compete with Kestrel Labs, especially as Marlow starts pitching smaller vehicles to civilian agencies. But the cultures are very different. At Kestrel the question is always "what does the scientist need?" rather than "what does the contract say?"

**What does a lead engineer on Project Sentinel actually do?**

I own the vehicle roadmap for the Sentinel fleet: reliability, endurance, and how the drones cope with the currents around Halden Reef. Day to day that means a lot of time with the Tidewater Institute scientists, who tell us what the data needs to look like, and with Vehl, who still knows the acoustic system better than anyone. Right now my priority is getting deployments from three weeks to six weeks without a recovery.

**What surprised you most?**

How much the drones depend on a few parts we don't control. At a big company you have whole teams managing suppliers. Here, if one component is late, the fleet is late.

**What's next for you?**

More drones in the water. We are going from four Sentinel units to twelve, and Mira Okafor has been clear that the fleet has to be boringly reliable before we expand to other reefs. I'm happy with boring.

Priya Nair holds a master's degree in ocean engineering and lives in Port Avalon with her partner and a very old cat.
